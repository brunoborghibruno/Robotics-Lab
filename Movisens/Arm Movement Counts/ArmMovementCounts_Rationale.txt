ARM MOVEMENT COUNTS - rationale (Bruno Borghi, October 2026)

Why not smoothness
SPARC (smoothness) from the same free-living wrist recordings did not change across Visits 1, 4 and 7, and EF
weeks did not differ from SHAM weeks (paired Wilcoxon p = 0.43, 14 subjects). At home, a "movement" can be
brushing teeth, walking or gesturing, and that mix changes from day to day more than movement quality does.
These are also chronic patients, who usually have little room left to change in smoothness.
Details in ../SPARC Analysis/.

Why "how much and how hard"
Studies of daily-life arm use after stroke mostly measure how often, how long and how strongly the affected arm
moves, not how smoothly (Lang lab: hours of use, movement magnitude, use ratio). The question that matters for
the patient is whether therapy makes them use the affected arm more in daily life. Gains in the clinic do not
always reach daily life (Waddell et al. 2017), so this is a separate, harder test of the treatment.
The sensor is on the affected wrist, so a higher value means more use of the affected arm. With one wrist
only, the use ratio between the two arms cannot be computed.

How we get there
The gyroscope gives the wrist's angular speed directly (no gravity to remove, unlike the accelerometer).
For each visit, in the first 7.4 h of recording (maxSamples, can be set to "all"):
  1. Convert to deg/s (0.07 deg/s per count), remove each axis's bias (median), take the speed (norm of the
     3 axes) and low-pass it at 10 Hz.
  2. Find the movement events (as in SPARC_Analysis.m): speed reaches 30 deg/s, edges at 10 deg/s, 0.5-10 s long.
  3. Three numbers:
       events per hour  = how often the arm moves
       active time (%)  = share of time with speed above 10 deg/s (how long it moves)
       intensity        = median peak speed of the events in deg/s (how hard it moves)
  4. Change between visits (Visit 4 - Visit 1, Visit 7 - Visit 4; positive = more use). Each subject has one
     EF week and one SHAM week, so each subject's EF change is compared with its own SHAM change
     (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank).

Main sources
  Lang CE et al. J Vis Exp 2017 (method for wrist accelerometry in daily life).
  Bailey RR, Klaesner JW, Lang CE. Neurorehabil Neural Repair 2015 (real-world arm activity after stroke).
  Waddell KJ et al. Neurorehabil Neural Repair 2017 (clinic gains vs daily-life use).
